Computer Engineering at the University of the East

Program overview, skills, labs, and the student experience — organized for quick scanning.

Accuracy note: Program structures evolve. This page describes the typical shape of a BS CpE program (Philippine CHED-aligned) for orientation. Confirm the current curriculum, admission steps, and campus offerings via official UE admissions and the College of Engineering.
Overview

A balanced engineering education: circuits, systems, and software

The BS in Computer Engineering at UE prepares students to analyze, design, and implement computing systems. Coursework typically spans mathematics and sciences, core engineering, computer hardware (digital logic, microprocessors, architecture), and computing (programming, data structures, operating systems, networks, databases) — capped by design projects where teams deliver a working system.

Program objectives (typical themes)

Technical competence

Apply math, science, and engineering to solve computing problems in hardware and software.

Professional practice

Work in teams, communicate clearly, and act ethically in real engineering contexts.

Growth & leadership

Pursue licensure, graduate study, or industry roles with a habit of lifelong learning.

Service

Use technology responsibly for community and national development.

Skills you will develop

C / C++ Python Digital logic Microcontrollers Networking Databases & OS Troubleshooting Technical writing Teamwork

🔧 Build

Prototype with dev boards, solder and wire carefully, read datasheets.

💻 Code

Write clean firmware and software; use Git, testing, and debugging tools.

📊 Validate

Measure, test edge cases, document results like an engineer.

Learning experiences

Foundation years

Calculus, physics, programming fundamentals, and introductory electronics — the shared language of engineering.

Core CpE years

Data structures, digital design, microprocessors, computer architecture, operating systems, networks, and software engineering.

Capstone & design

Team-based design project: proposal, prototype iterations, testing, and final defense/exhibit. See illustrative project examples.

Beyond the classroom

SCPES workshops, seminars, competitions, and peer mentoring. See SCPES.

Laboratories & facilities Typical
  • Electronics & circuits lab (bench instruments, components)
  • Digital logic & microprocessor lab (trainers, dev boards)
  • Computer & networking lab (structured cabling, routers/switches)
  • Project/maker space for capstone builds

Exact rooms and equipment vary by campus and semester — ask the department during a visit.

Research & innovation directions

Student-friendly entry points: IoT sensing, assistive devices, campus automation, applied ML, and network measurement. Pair with a project idea and a faculty adviser.
