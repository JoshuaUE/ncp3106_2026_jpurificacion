Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers

Computer Engineering (CpE) sits at the intersection of hardware and software — you learn digital logic, computer architecture, embedded systems, firmware, and low-level software. You can design a processor and write the OS that runs on it.

Computer Science (CS) focuses on algorithms, theory, software engineering, AI/ML, and large-scale systems. Less hardware, more abstraction.

Electronics Engineering (ECE) covers broader electronics: analog/digital circuits, communications, signal processing, power, and control. CpE is a specialized subset focused on computing systems.

It's genuinely both. Expect serious programming (C/C++, Python, data structures, OS, databases) and serious hardware (digital logic, microprocessors, electronics, PCB design). The ratio varies by elective track — you can lean embedded (more hardware) or systems software (more code) — but the core curriculum requires competence in both domains.

Admission requirements change by academic year and campus (Manila vs. Caloocan). Typically:

  • Senior High School graduate (STEM strand preferred)
  • UE College Admission Test (UECAT) or qualifying exam
  • Minimum grade requirements in Math, Science, English
  • Interview or portfolio review (varies by year)

Always check the current UE Admissions page for the official, up-to-date requirements, deadlines, and procedures.

Yes. BS CpE graduates take the Computer Engineering Licensure Examination administered by the PRC (Professional Regulation Commission). It covers Mathematics, Electronics, Computer Engineering (hardware/software), and General Engineering & Applied Sciences.

UE's historical passing rates vary by year. The department runs dedicated review programs. For the latest official passing rates and topnotcher lists, check the PRC website or the UE College of Engineering announcements.

Core curriculum typically includes:

  • C/C++ — firmware, systems, embedded (primary)
  • Python — scripting, data, ML, automation
  • Verilog/VHDL — digital design, FPGA
  • Assembly — architecture, reverse engineering
  • Java/C# — OOP, applications (varies)

Tools: Git, Linux, GCC/Clang, CMake, VS Code, Quartus/Vivado, LTspice, Wireshark, oscilloscopes, logic analyzers. Electives add: Rust, Go, MATLAB, ROS 2, TensorFlow/PyTorch, Kubernetes.

Yes, multiple sources:

  • UE Scholarships — academic, athletic, cultural, leadership (check UE Scholarship Office)
  • DOST-SEI — RA 7687, RA 10612, Merit (for STEM students)
  • CHED Tulong Dunong — financial assistance for qualified students
  • Industry scholarships — semiconductor, telco, and tech firms (often via SCPES/college linkages)
  • Private foundations — various STEM-focused grants

Visit the UE Scholarship Office and monitor SCPES announcements for application windows.

Typical facilities (exact rooms/equipment vary by campus and semester):

  • Electronics & Circuits Lab — oscilloscopes, function generators, power supplies, component stocks
  • Digital Logic & Microprocessor Lab — FPGA boards (Xilinx/Intel), microcontroller trainers (ARM, PIC, AVR)
  • Computer & Networking Lab — structured cabling, routers/switches, Linux servers, virtualization
  • Project/Maker Space — 3D printers, soldering stations, bench tools, component library
  • Research Lab — higher-end equipment for faculty-led projects (varies)

Ask for a lab tour during campus visits or SCPES open house events.

Yes, shifting is possible subject to:

  • Availability of slots in the CpE program
  • Grade requirements (typically minimum GWA, no failing grades)
  • Completion of prerequisite courses (Calculus, Physics, Programming fundamentals)
  • Interview with the department chair or program adviser

Credited units are evaluated course-by-course. Consult the UE Registrar and the CpE Department for the current shifting policy and application timeline.

SCPES (Student Council of Computer Engineering Students) is the official student organization for BS CpE majors. It runs workshops, hackathons, competitions, industry talks, peer tutoring, and community events.

All CpE students are automatically members. To get active: attend a General Assembly (monthly), join a committee (Tech, Events, Externals, etc.), or volunteer at an event. No fees, no interviews — just show up. See the SCPES page for details.

  • UE Student Portal (LMS) — current syllabi, schedules, grades
  • College of Engineering Office — curriculum maps, prerequisite charts
  • ue.edu.ph — official announcements, downloadable forms
